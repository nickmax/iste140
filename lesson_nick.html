Lesson 2: Game Engines & Graphics Rendering

Author: Nick (CSS Lead)

1. What Does a Game Engine Do?

A game engine (such as Unreal Engine or Unity) is the core software architecture running a game. It manages graphics rendering, audio pipelines, physics collisions, memory usage, and user input simultaneously.

2. The Rendering Pipeline (CPU to GPU)

Every single frame displayed on your screen goes through a rapid multi-step processing pipeline:

  1. CPU Simulation: Calculates character coordinates, collision boundaries, and artificial intelligence decisions.
  2. Draw Calls: The CPU sends instructions telling the GPU what 3D meshes and materials exist in the scene.
  3. GPU Rasterization: The GPU calculates lighting, shadows, shaders, and camera perspectives, turning 3D mathematical vectors into 2D pixels on your screen.

3. Client-Side Graphics vs. Server-Side Logic

Game servers do not stream video to your computer. Your local device renders all high-definition graphics stored on your hard drive, while the server only broadcasts light numerical coordinates (x, y, z positions).
