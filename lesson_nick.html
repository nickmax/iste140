Lesson 2: Game Engines & Graphics Rendering

Author: Nick (CSS Lead)

1. What Does a Game Engine Do?

A game engine, such as Unreal Engine or Unity, is the software that runs a game. It handles graphics and sound, along with physics, memory and player input.

2. The Rendering Pipeline (CPU to GPU)

Each frame on your screen goes through these steps:

  1. CPU Simulation: Works out character positions, collisions and decisions for computer-controlled characters.
  2. Draw Calls: The CPU tells the GPU which 3D objects and materials to draw.
  3. GPU Rasterization: The GPU uses the scene geometry, camera settings and shaders to produce the pixels you see on screen.

3. Client-Side Graphics vs. Server-Side Logic

Game servers do not stream video to your computer. Your local device renders all high-definition graphics stored on your hard drive, while the server only broadcasts light numerical coordinates (x, y, z positions).
